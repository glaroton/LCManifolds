# lc-manifolds

**Manifolds modeled on locally convex spaces, formalized in Lean 4 and Mathlib.**

API documentation (generated by doc-gen4): <https://glaroton.github.io/lc-manifolds/docs/>

> **Status: in development.** The library contains manifolds, smooth maps, the tangent bundle,
> vector fields with the Lie bracket, connections, sprays, geodesics, submanifolds and immersions,
> connections defined by fields of projectors, and a collection of examples. It is the geometric
> companion of [lc-analysis](https://github.com/glaroton/lc-analysis), which provides the calculus.

## Convention: model spaces need not be complete

Model spaces are Hausdorff locally convex spaces, **not assumed complete**, as in Glöckner–Neeb
(following Glöckner 2002); Milnor (1984) assumed sequential completeness instead. The formal
theory does not need completeness. Results that do need it (integrals in the model space,
differential equations, exponential maps, inverse function theorems) state it as a hypothesis.

In particular, a manifold modeled on a normed space is **not** a Banach manifold unless that space
is complete, and one modeled on an inner product space is **not** a Hilbert manifold unless that
space is complete. Every statement about Banach or Hilbert manifolds here carries the
completeness hypothesis. Without completeness things go wrong: Glöckner–Neeb (Example 5.6.8)
give a Lie group modeled on an incomplete normed algebra with no exponential function.

## Contents

* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Basic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Basic.lean):
  * `mbGroupoid E`, the structure groupoid of Michal–Bastiani smooth diffeomorphisms between open
    subsets of a locally convex space `E`;
  * `MBManifold`, smooth manifolds modeled on `E` [Nee06, Def. I.3.1; GN26, Ch. 3];
  * `BanachManifold`, `HilbertManifold`: the case of a complete normed, resp. complete inner
    product, model space (these name the completeness hypothesis explicitly).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Product.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Product.lean): products. `M × N` is a manifold
  modeled on `E × F` (`Prod.mbManifold`); the projections are smooth and pairs of `C^k` maps
  are `C^k`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Connection.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Connection.lean): connections and covariant
  derivatives. A `Connection` is a family of Christoffel symbols `Γᵢ`, one per chart, with the
  change of charts law `Γⱼ(τ x)(dτ a, dτ b) = dτ Γᵢ(x)(a, b) + d²τₓ(a, b)`; conventions as in
  Lang (first argument the direction of motion, Lang's sign for the curvature).
  * `covDeriv`: `∇_X Y = dỸ · X̃ − Γ(X̃, Ỹ)`; `localRep_covDeriv`: it does not depend on the
    chart (the `d²τ` terms cancel);
  * `covDeriv_smulField_left`, `covDeriv_smulField_right`: `∇_{φX} Y = φ ∇_X Y`,
    `∇_X (φY) = X(φ) Y + φ ∇_X Y`;
  * torsion: `∇_X Y − ∇_Y X − [X, Y] = Γ(Y, X) − Γ(X, Y)` pointwise, and
    `∇_X Y − ∇_Y X = [X, Y]` for symmetric connections (sprays);
  * `contMDiffMB_covDeriv`: `∇_X Y` is `C^k` if `X` is `C^k` and `Y` is `C^(k+1)`;
  * `curvature_eq`: **the curvature is tensorial**. `R(X, Y) Z = ∇_X ∇_Y Z − ∇_Y ∇_X Z −
    ∇_[X,Y] Z` (defined through vector fields, so it does not depend on the chart) is, in a chart,
    the trilinear expression `dΓ(v)(u, w) − dΓ(u)(v, w) + Γ(u, Γ(v, w)) − Γ(v, Γ(u, w))` (Lang,
    Prop. IX.1.2); `curvature_swap`, `curvature_bianchi`: skew-symmetry and Bianchi's identity.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/CovariantCurve.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/CovariantCurve.lean): covariant derivatives
  along curves and geodesics.
  * `covDerivAlong`: for a lift `β` of a curve `α`, `D_t β = μ̇ − Γ(α̃)(α̃̇, μ)` in a chart;
    `covDerivAlong_chart`: it does not depend on the chart;
  * `velocity`, `isCkLift_velocity`: the velocity of a `C^(k+1)` curve is a `C^k` lift;
  * `IsGeodesic` (`D_t α' = 0`) and `isGeodesic_iff`: the geodesic equation
    `α̃̈ = Γ(α̃)(α̃̇, α̃̇)` in every chart. Existence of geodesics is not claimed (it needs ODE
    theory, which fails in general locally convex spaces).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/DoubleTangent.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/DoubleTangent.lean): the double tangent bundle
  `TTM = T(TM)`, with charts `((x, v), (u, w))` over charts of `M`.
  * `tangentChart_transition_apply`: the transition maps of `TM` are `(x, v) ↦ (τ x, dτₓ v)`;
  * `ddChart_transition_apply`: those of `TTM` are
    `((x, v), (u, w)) ↦ ((τ x, dτₓ v), (dτₓ u, d²τₓ(u, v) + dτₓ w))`;
  * `flip`, the canonical flip `((x, v), (u, w)) ↦ ((x, u), (v, w))`: `ddChart_flip` shows it
    does not depend on the chart (the transition maps commute with it by Schwarz), and
    `flip_flip` that it is an involution.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Spray.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Spray.lean): sprays, by their local parts
  `fᵢ(x, v)` with the change of charts law `fⱼ(τ x, dτ v) = dτ fᵢ(x, v) + d²τₓ(v, v)`.
  * `Connection.toSpray`: a connection gives a quadratic spray, `f(x, v) = Γₓ(v, v)`;
  * `Spray.toConnection`: a quadratic spray gives a symmetric connection, by polarization
    `Γₓ(a, b) = ¼ (f(x, a + b) − f(x, a − b))`, and `toConnection_toSpray` recovers the spray.
    The key facts: a map homogeneous of degree 2 is half its second derivative at `0` on the
    diagonal (`dMB_dMB_zero_of_homogeneous`, no regularity needed), and the second derivative
    of the transition maps polarizes.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Submanifold.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Submanifold.lean): embedded submanifolds.
  `N ⊆ M` is embedded with model a closed subspace `S ⊆ E` if every point lies in a slice chart,
  `φ(U ∩ N) = φ(U) ∩ S`; no complement of `S` is required. Then `N` is a manifold modeled on `S`
  (`IsEmbeddedSubmanifold.mbManifold`) and the inclusion is smooth. The key calculus fact is
  `ContDiffMBOn.codRestrict`: a `C^k` map with values in a closed subspace is `C^k` into it.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/SubmanifoldNotions.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/SubmanifoldNotions.lean): weak
  immersions (injective differential), immersions (differential a topological isomorphism onto
  a closed subspace) and split immersions (with a continuous linear left inverse); weakly
  immersed, immersed and initial submanifolds (Glöckner–Neeb, Def. 3.3.22); split submanifolds.
  The inclusion of an embedded submanifold is an immersion and is initial
  (`IsEmbeddedSubmanifold.isInitialSubmanifold_val`, via `ContDiffMBOn.codRestrict`), and a split
  immersion for split submanifolds. The module docstring lists the infinite-dimensional
  subtleties: the cusp (Joris), the figure eight, a topologically embedded weak immersion of `ℓ²`
  that is not initial, unitary orbits, and an initial submanifold that is not an immersion.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/ImmersionBanach.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/ImmersionBanach.lean): if the domain is a
  Banach manifold (model complete normed) and the target is modeled on a normed space (complete or
  not), an immersion (no complement of the range assumed) reflects smoothness of continuous maps
  from manifolds modeled on any locally convex space (`IsImmersion.smoothMBM_of_comp`); hence an
  injective immersion that is a homeomorphism onto its image is an initial submanifold
  (`IsImmersedSubmanifold.isInitialSubmanifold`). The calculus is
  `LocallyConvex.Analysis.ContDiffMBOn.of_comp_of_boundedBelow`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Projector.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Projector.lean): derive and project (Larotonda,
  *Estructuras*, Teorema *esproyectar*). For an embedded submanifold `X ⊆ F` modeled on `S`, the
  tangent spaces `T_p X ⊆ F` (read in any slice chart), fields of projectors `E : X × F → F`
  onto them (`ProjectorField`), and the connection they define (`ProjectorField.connection`):
  in a slice chart with `ψ = φ⁻¹|_S`, `Γ_y(a, b) = - dφ (E (d²ψ_y(a, b)))`. It is symmetric by
  Schwarz, and the change of charts law follows from the second-order chain rule
  (`LocallyConvex.Analysis.dMB_dMB_comp`). The covariant derivative is the projection of the ambient
  derivative (`dMB_param_localCovDeriv`), and the geodesics are the curves with normal
  acceleration, `E_α (α̈) = 0` (`connection_isGeodesic_iff`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/Finsler.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/Finsler.lean): Finsler seminorms
  (continuous, subadditive, positively homogeneous; not necessarily symmetric nor definite) and
  two notions on a manifold, kept apart by name. A **Finsler metric** is continuous on the
  tangent bundle; a **Finsler length structure** is only continuous along `C¹` curves, which is
  what lengths need and what quotient metrics on homogeneous spaces satisfy (Larotonda, Forum
  Math. 2019, Lemma 3.19). Every Finsler metric is a Finsler length structure, because velocities
  of `C¹` curves are continuous in `TM` (`continuous_velocity`). Length of curves and the Finsler
  distance are defined for Finsler length structures, the distance over piecewise `C¹` paths
  (chains of `C¹` arcs, `Reach`). It satisfies the **triangle inequality** (`dist_triangle`), so
  it is an extended, possibly asymmetric, pseudo-distance, and it is symmetric when `μ` is
  reversible (`dist_comm`, by reversing curves).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/LengthMetric.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/LengthMetric.lean):
  metric spaces after Gromov (Larotonda, *Estructuras*, Cap. 8). Lengths of continuous curves
  (`eVariationOn`), the rectifiable distance `d_ℓ ≥ d` and the criterion for equal topologies
  (Lema 8.1.8), interior metric spaces and approximate midpoints.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/HopfRinow.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/HopfRinow.lean): the
  metric Hopf–Rinow theorem (*Estructuras*, Teorema 8.2.3): in a complete, locally compact interior
  space closed balls are compact and any two points are joined by a shortest curve; Lipschitz
  reparametrization by arc length (Obs. 8.1.5).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/AsymmDist.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/AsymmDist.lean),
  [`ForwardLength.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/ForwardLength.lean),
  [`ForwardHopfRinow.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/ForwardHopfRinow.lean): asymmetric distances
  after Mennucci (Anal. Geom. Metr. Spaces 2013), with the topology of the symmetrized distance:
  forward lengths, continuity of the running length, forward-interior spaces, and shortest forward
  curves under compactness and bounded asymmetry.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerMetricSpace.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerMetricSpace.lean): for a
  reversible Finsler length structure, `(M, d_b)` is a pseudo-metric space in which `C¹` curves
  are continuous, `ℓ(γ) ≤ L_b(γ)`, and which is an interior metric space (*Estructuras*,
  Prop. 9.1.2).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerSymmetrization.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerSymmetrization.lean):
  the symmetrization `μ(v) + μ(-v)`; with a reversibility constant it gives the same topology as
  `d_b`, but not the same lengths or shortest curves.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerBounded.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerBounded.lean): Finsler
  structures bounded above and below on a normed model (*Estructuras*, Def. 9.1.3). Leaving a
  chart costs length (complete model, Hausdorff manifold); `d_b` separates points and every open
  set of `M` is `d_b`-open (Lema 9.1.8); `d_b ≤ ‖φ y − φ x‖ / m` for structures bounded above, so
  strong Finsler manifolds are homeomorphic to `(M, d_b)` (Cor. 9.1.9); in finite dimension every
  Finsler metric is strong (Teorema 9.1.5). On non-normable models strong Finsler structures do
  not exist.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerHopfRinow.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerHopfRinow.lean):
  Hopf–Rinow for finite-dimensional Finsler manifolds (*Estructuras*, Cor. 9.1.10): if `(M, d_b)`
  is complete, any two points are joined by a continuous shortest curve.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerQuotient.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerQuotient.lean): where
  the two continuity conditions come from, for a field `μ` on `M` controlled through a `C¹` map
  `π : N → M` (typically a quotient metric). If curves lift locally and `μ ∘ Tπ` is continuous
  along curves, `μ` is a Finsler length structure; if `Tπ` is a quotient map and `μ ∘ Tπ` is
  continuous, `μ` is a Finsler metric. Includes the chain rule `Tπ(Γ̇) = (π ∘ Γ)˙`
  (`tangentMap_velocity`) and a discussion of Banach submersions versus homogeneous spaces of
  locally convex Lie groups.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/Bilinear.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/Bilinear.lean): smooth fields of
  symmetric bilinear forms, by signature. **Weak Riemannian** metrics (Schmeding, Def. 4.1.1;
  "weak" refers to the topology of the fibers, `IsStrongAt` to the strong case) are Finsler
  metrics, via Cauchy–Schwarz (`WeakRiemannianMetric.toFinslerMetric`). **Fundamental
  decompositions** `E₊ ⊕ E₋` and the **index**, well defined as an algebraic dimension without
  any completeness (`FundamentalDecomposition.rank_neg_eq`). **Weak pseudo-Riemannian** metrics of
  arbitrary index (finite: Pontryagin type; infinite: Krein type) are weakly nondegenerate;
  **weak Lorentzian** metrics have index `1`, and a timelike vector with positive definite
  orthogonal gives a topological decomposition `ℝ v ⊕ v^⊥` (`FundamentalDecomposition.ofTimelike`).
  Lorentzian and pseudo-Riemannian metrics are not Finsler metrics.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/LeviCivita.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/LeviCivita.lean): metric
  connections for fields of symmetric bilinear forms of any signature. The **Koszul formula**
  (`Connection.koszul`), **uniqueness** of the Levi-Civita connection for weakly nondegenerate
  metrics, in particular weak pseudo-Riemannian metrics of any index
  (`Connection.Γ_eq_of_isMetric`, `WeakPseudoRiemannianMetric.covDeriv_eq`), and the **metric
  spray**: a symmetric connection is metric if and only if its spray satisfies
  `G(F(v), z) = −∂G(v)(v, z) + ½ ∂G(z)(v, v)` in every chart (`Connection.isMetric_iff_spray`;
  Larotonda, *Estructuras*, Teorema 10.1.1). Existence is not claimed: for weak metrics the
  Koszul functional need not be represented (`exists_symm_compatible_iff`; Schmeding's
  Example 4.3.7 on `ℓ²`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/NoLeviCivita.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/NoLeviCivita.lean): the
  obstruction happens. For a symmetric positive definite operator `T` and `v ∉ T(H)`, the weak
  Riemannian metric `g_p(x, y) = e^{⟨v, p⟩} ⟨T x, y⟩` has no symmetric metric connection
  (`NoLeviCivita.not_exists_isMetric`); on `ℓ²`, `T x = (x_n / (n + 1))_n` and
  `v = (1 / (n + 1))_n` do it (`NoLeviCivita.L2.not_exists_isMetric`, after Schmeding,
  Example 4.3.7).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/ProjectorMetric.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/ProjectorMetric.lean):
  submanifolds of a space with a continuous symmetric form `β` of any signature (inner product,
  Krein, Pontryagin). If the field of projectors is `β`-orthogonal, its connection is metric for
  the induced form (`ProjectorField.isMetric_symb`, Gauss) and is the Levi-Civita connection
  (`ProjectorField.symb_eq_of_isMetric`). The induced forms glue to a metric on `X`
  (`ProjectorField.inducedMetric`, weak Riemannian when `β` is positive definite), and globally
  every symmetric metric connection of it is the projector connection
  (`ProjectorField.covDeriv_eq_connection`). Orthogonal projectors only exist onto nondegenerate
  subspaces (`nondegenerate_of_orthogonal`), which is what fails for lightlike submanifolds; in
  Krein spaces the exact condition is Bognár, Theorem V.3.4 (not formalized).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/FinslerCusp.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/FinslerCusp.lean): the two
  notions differ already on `ℝ²`. With `β(x, y) = max(0, x² - |y|) / (x² + |y|)`, the Finsler
  norms `μ_p(v) = ‖v‖ + β(p) |v₂|` are continuous along every `C¹` curve (a curve through the
  origin with `γ̇₂ ≠ 0` leaves the cusp `|y| < x²`) but not on the tangent bundle
  (`CuspFinsler.lengthStructure`, `CuspFinsler.not_continuous`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/McAlpin.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/McAlpin.lean): McAlpin's example
  (*Estructuras*, Ejemplo 10.4.2): a closed submanifold `M = A(S)` of `ℓ²` in which `e₀` and `-e₀`
  are at distance `π` but no piecewise `C¹` curve joining them has length `π`. In infinite
  dimension Hopf–Rinow fails.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Subspace.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Subspace.lean): example. A closed
  subspace is an embedded submanifold (split if complemented), with tangent space itself; a
  continuous projection onto it is a constant field of projectors, whose connection is flat and
  whose geodesics are the curves with zero acceleration. The linear inclusion of a closed
  subspace is an immersion, and it is a split immersion exactly when the subspace is
  complemented (`isImmersion_subtypeL`, `isSplitImmersion_subtypeL_iff`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/OpenAndGraph.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/OpenAndGraph.lean): examples.
  Open subsets are embedded submanifolds (smooth charts can be restricted to open sets). The
  graph of a smooth map is a split submanifold, with the explicit slice chart
  `(x, y) ↦ (x, y - g x)`: no inverse function theorem is needed.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Sphere.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Sphere.lean): example. The unit
  sphere of a real inner product space is an embedded submanifold, with explicit slice charts on
  hemispheres moved by Householder reflections (no inverse function theorem); `T_p S = p^⊥`;
  the orthogonal projections form a field of projectors, whose geodesics are the curves with
  normal acceleration, among them the great circles. It is a Hilbert manifold exactly when the
  inner product space is complete.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Counterexamples.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Counterexamples.lean):
  counterexamples to the implications between submanifold notions. The inclusion `ℓ¹ → ℓ²`
  (continuous by the closed graph theorem) is a weak immersion but not an immersion
  (`isWeakImmersion_lpInclusion`, `not_isImmersion_lpInclusion`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Phillips.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Phillips.lean): Phillips' lemma,
  `c₀` is not complemented in `ℓ^∞` (`Phillips.not_closedComplemented_c0`), with Whitley's proof
  through an uncountable almost disjoint family of subsets of `ℕ`. Hence `c₀ ⊆ ℓ^∞` is an
  embedded submanifold that is not split, and the inclusion `c₀ → ℓ^∞` is an immersion that is
  not a split immersion (`Phillips.isImmersion_c0`, `Phillips.not_isSplitImmersion_c0`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Lemniscate.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Lemniscate.lean): the figure
  eight `t ↦ (sin θ, sin 2θ)`, `θ = 2 arctan t`, is an injectively immersed submanifold of `ℝ²`
  (`isImmersedSubmanifold_lemniscate`) that is not initial (`not_isInitialSubmanifold_lemniscate`):
  a smooth curve crossing the origin along the other branch has a lift that jumps.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Unitary.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/Unitary.lean): example. The
  unitary group of a real Banach algebra with continuous involution (for instance `B(H)`, `H` a
  complex Hilbert space) is a split submanifold modeled on the skew-adjoint elements
  (`isSplitSubmanifold_unitary`, `isSplitSubmanifold_unitary_clm`), with explicit Cayley slice
  charts `a ↦ (a - 1)(a + 1)⁻¹` and no inverse function theorem; `T_p U = p · 𝔲`
  (`tangentSpace_unitary`). The projectors `E_p w = (w - p w* p) / 2` form a field of projectors
  (`projectorField_unitary`); its geodesics satisfy `α̈ = α α̈* α` (`isGeodesic_unitary_iff`), and
  the curves `t ↦ p exp(t X)` are geodesics (`isGeodesic_expCurve`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/PeriodicSmooth.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/PeriodicSmooth.lean): the
  space `C^∞_per` of smooth `1`-periodic functions, with the topology of uniform convergence of
  all derivatives (seminorms `sup |u^{(k)}|`), is a Fréchet space: locally convex, Hausdorff,
  metrizable and complete (`CInfPer.tendsto_iff`, `CInfPer.isClosed_range_J`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/DiffCircle.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/DiffCircle.lean): `Diff⁺(S¹)`,
  through its lifts `id + u`, as the open subset `Ω = {u | 1 + u' > 0}` of `C^∞_per`
  (`DiffCircle.isOpen_domain`), hence a Fréchet (not Banach) manifold. For `u ∈ Ω`, `id + u` is a
  diffeomorphism of `ℝ`, and `Ω` is closed under inversion and composition (`DiffCircle.inv_mem`,
  `DiffCircle.comp_mem`). Conversely, every diffeomorphism of `ℝ` commuting with the translation
  by `1` is `id + u` for a unique `u ∈ Ω` (`DiffCircle.existsUnique_mem_domain_lift_eq`); in
  fact `u ∈ Ω` exactly when `id + u` has a differentiable left inverse
  (`DiffCircle.mem_domain_iff`). Smoothness of the group operations is left to the Lie group
  phase.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/ContMDiff.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/ContMDiff.lean): `C^k` and smooth maps between
  manifolds (`ContMDiffMBOn`, `SmoothMBMOn`, `SmoothMBM`), defined through the local
  representatives in the preferred charts:
  * `ContMDiffMBOn.contDiffMBOn_chart`: the local representatives in *any* charts of the atlases
    are `C^k`, since transition maps are smooth;
  * `ContMDiffMBOn.comp`: compositions of `C^k` maps are `C^k`;
  * identities and constant maps are smooth, and between the model spaces the notion agrees with
    the Michal–Bastiani one (`contMDiffMBOn_iff_contDiffMBOn`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/TangentBundle.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/TangentBundle.lean): the tangent bundle.
  * `tangentBundleCore`: a fiber bundle core indexed by the charts of the atlas, whose coordinate
    changes are the derivatives of the transition maps (the cocycle condition is the chain rule);
  * `TangentBundle E M`, a topological fiber bundle with fiber `E`, charted on `E × E`;
  * `TangentBundle.mbManifold`: it is a manifold. Its transition maps are
    `(y, w) ↦ (τ y, dτ(y) w)`, which are smooth because `τ` is.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/TangentMap.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/TangentMap.lean): the tangent map `Tf : TM → TN`.
  * `dMB_chart_comp`, `dMB_comp_chart`: derivatives of local representatives change by the
    derivatives of the transition maps;
  * `tangentMap_chart`: in the charts of the tangent bundles, `Tf` reads `(y, w) ↦ (h y, dh(y) w)`
    for the local representative `h` of `f`;
  * `ContMDiffMB.tangentMap`: if `f` is `C^(k+1)`, then `Tf` is `C^k`;
  * `tangentMap_id`, `tangentMap_comp`: `T id = id` and `T(g ∘ f) = Tg ∘ Tf`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/VectorField.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/VectorField.lean): vector fields and the Lie
  bracket.
  * `localRep_transition`: local representatives transform by `X̃ⱼ ∘ τ = dτ · X̃ᵢ`;
  * `lieBracket`: defined in the preferred chart at each point by the local formula
    `dỸ · X̃ − dX̃ · Ỹ`;
  * `localRep_lieBracket`: **the bracket does not depend on the chart**. In infinite dimensions
    vector fields need not have flows, so the usual proof through the Lie derivative is not
    available; the proof is direct (`localBracket_transition`): differentiating
    `X̃ⱼ ∘ τ = dτ · X̃ᵢ` produces `d²τ(X̃ᵢ, Ỹᵢ)`, which cancels by the symmetry of second
    derivatives;
  * `lieBracket_swap`: antisymmetry;
  * `contMDiffMB_of_localRep`: a vector field with `C^k` local representatives is `C^k` as a map
    `M → TM`; `contMDiffMB_lieBracket`: if `X, Y` are `C^(k+1)`, then `[X, Y]` is `C^k`;
  * `lieBracket_jacobi`: the Jacobi identity for `C^2` fields (`localBracket_jacobi` in a chart:
    the second derivatives cancel by symmetry, the first-order terms by linearity);
  * `lieBracket_related`: if `X, Y` are `f`-related to `X', Y'` (`Tf ∘ X = X' ∘ f`), then
    `[X, Y]` is `f`-related to `[X', Y']` (Lang, Prop. V.1.4), for `f` of class `C^2`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Derivation.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Derivation.lean): vector fields acting on
  functions.
  * `vfDeriv X φ`, the derivative `X(φ)` of a `C^1` function (`IsCkFunction`), and
    `vfDeriv_chart`: it does not depend on the chart; `vfDeriv_mul`: Leibniz rule;
  * `lieBracket_smulField`: `[X, φY] = X(φ) Y + φ [X, Y]`;
  * `eq_of_vfDeriv_clm_comp_chart`: a vector field is determined at `x` by its action on the
    local functions `λ ∘ c`, `λ` continuous linear (Hahn–Banach). Smooth bump functions need not
    exist, so global functions do not suffice in general.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Analytic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Analytic.lean): real and complex
  analytic manifolds [GN26, Defs. 3.1.3–3.1.4].
  * `realAnalyticGroupoid`, `complexAnalyticGroupoid`, with real analyticity in the sense of
    Milnor (extension to a complex analytic map on the complexification);
  * `RealAnalyticManifold`, `ComplexAnalyticManifold`: complex analytic manifolds are real
    analytic, and real analytic manifolds are smooth;
  * `RealAnalyticMOn`, `ComplexAnalyticMOn`: analytic maps between manifolds, closed under
    composition; `IsComplexification`: complexifications of real analytic manifolds
    [GN26, Def. 2.2.18].
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/LocalForm.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/LocalForm.lean): differential
  forms on open subsets of locally convex spaces [GN26, §3.6].
  * `IsLocalForm`: smooth maps `U × Eⁿ → F`, alternating `n`-linear in the vectors;
  * `dLoc`: the exterior derivative `dω(y)(v₀, …, vₙ) = ∑ᵢ (-1)ⁱ ∂_{vᵢ} ω(y)(…, v̂ᵢ, …)`
    [GN26, (3.33)]; `IsLocalForm.dLoc_dLoc`: `d ∘ d = 0`;
  * `IsLocalForm.dLoc_pullbackLoc`: naturality `d(τ*ω) = τ*(dω)` under smooth changes of
    variables; the terms with `d²τ` cancel because `d²τ` is symmetric.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/DifferentialForm.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/DifferentialForm.lean):
  differential forms on manifolds [GN26, §3.6].
  * `DifferentialForm E M F n`: smooth `F`-valued `n`-forms, defined by their local
    representatives [GN26, Def. 3.6.1];
  * `DifferentialForm.d`: the exterior derivative; `localRep_d`: in every chart it is given by
    the local formula, so it does not depend on the charts [GN26, Prop. 3.6.7];
    `d_d`: `d ∘ d = 0` [GN26, Prop. 3.6.9];
  * `ofFunction`, `d_ofFunction_apply`: functions as `0`-forms, and `df` is the differential
    [GN26, Rem. 3.6.12]; `IsClosed`, `IsExact`, `IsExact.isClosed`;
  * `pullback`: pullbacks along smooth maps [GN26, Def. 3.6.6], and `d_pullback`:
    `d(φ*ω) = φ*(dω)` [GN26, Lemma 3.6.13]; `pullback_id`, `pullback_pullback` [GN26, (3.27)];
  * the vector space structure of `Ωⁿ(M, F)`, `dLinear`; `compCLM`, `d_compCLM`: composition
    with continuous linear maps commutes with `d` [GN26, Exercise 3.6.4].
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Wedge.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Wedge.lean): the wedge product
  with respect to a continuous bilinear map `β` [GN26, Def. 3.6.3].
  * `wedgeAlt`: the wedge product of alternating maps; `DifferentialForm.wedge`, `wedgeBilinear`;
  * `wedge_apply_one_one`: `(ω ∧ η)(v₁, v₂) = β(ω v₁, η v₂) − β(ω v₂, η v₁)`;
    `pullback_wedge`: `φ*(ω ∧ η) = φ*ω ∧ φ*η` [GN26, (3.28)];
  * `smulFun`: products `f ω` with smooth real functions, and `wedge_ofFunction`
    [GN26, Rem. 3.6.5].
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/WedgeLeibniz.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/WedgeLeibniz.lean): the
  Leibniz rule `d(ω ∧ η) = dω ∧ η + (-1)ᵖ ω ∧ dη` (`DifferentialForm.d_wedge`), reduced in charts
  to two identities between alternating maps, proved with the unnormalized alternation `altF`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/LieDerivative.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/LieDerivative.lean):
  insertion of vector fields and the Lie derivative [GN26, §3.6].
  * `SmoothVectorField`, `DifferentialForm.interior`: `i_X ω` [GN26, Def. 3.6.15];
  * `lieDeriv`: `L_X = d ∘ i_X + i_X ∘ d`; `localRep_lieDeriv`: the local formula
    [GN26, Def. 3.6.14];
  * `d_apply_fields`, `lieDeriv_apply_fields`: `dω` and `L_Y ω` evaluated on vector fields
    [GN26, (3.29), (3.35)];
  * `lieDeriv_lieDeriv_sub`, `lieDeriv_interior_sub`, `lieDeriv_d`, `IsClosed.isExact_lieDeriv`:
    [GN26, Prop. 3.6.16]; `pullback_interior`, `pullback_lieDeriv`: related vector fields
    [GN26, Exercise 3.6.6].
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/DeRham.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/DeRham.lean): de Rham cohomology.
  * `closedForms`, `exactForms`, `DeRham E M F n` [GN26, Def. 3.6.11];
  * `isClosed_iff_isLocallyConstant`, `deRhamZeroEquiv`: `H⁰` consists of the locally constant
    functions [GN26, Rem. 3.6.12 (b)];
  * `deRhamMap`, `deRhamMap_id`, `deRhamMap_comp`: functoriality of `H^n_dR`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Homotopy.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Homotopy.lean): the homotopy
  formula and the Poincaré Lemma [GN26, Lemma 3.6.25, Thm. 3.6.28, Cor. 3.6.29], for forms with
  values in Mackey complete spaces; homotopies are smooth maps on `ℝ × M`.
  * `DifferentialForm.fibInt`, `d_fibInt_add`: the fiber integral and
    `d I(ω) + I(dω) = i₁*ω − i₀*ω` [GN26, (3.40)];
  * `deRhamMap_homotopy`: homotopic maps induce the same maps in de Rham cohomology;
  * `IsClosed.isExact_of_smoothlyContractible`, `IsClosed.isExact_of_starConvex`: closed forms of
    positive degree on smoothly contractible manifolds, e.g. open star-shaped subsets of `E`, are
    exact.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/AnalyticForm.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/AnalyticForm.lean): real
  analytic forms on real analytic manifolds [GN26, Rem. 3.6.18].
  * `DifferentialForm.IsRealAnalytic`, `analyticForms`: the subspace `Ωⁿ_ω(M, F)`;
  * `IsRealAnalytic.d`, `.compCLM`, `.wedge`, `.pullback`, `.interior`, `.lieDeriv`: the
    constructions on forms preserve real analyticity (for real analytic maps and vector fields);
  * `AnalyticDeRham`, `AnalyticDeRham.toDeRham`: real analytic de Rham cohomology and its map to
    `H^n_dR`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic.lean): symplectic
  manifolds, with weak and strong nondegeneracy.
  * `SymplecticForm`, `SymplecticForm.IsStrong`; `IsStrong.surjective_inclusionInDoubleDual`: a
    normed model of a strong symplectic manifold is reflexive;
  * `IsHamiltonianFor`, `IsSymplecticField`: Hamiltonian fields are unique and symplectic;
    symplectic fields are Hamiltonian on smoothly contractible manifolds; brackets of symplectic
    fields are Hamiltonian; conservation of energy;
  * `poisson`, `poisson_jacobi`: the Poisson bracket and the Jacobi identity;
  * `IsSymplecticMap`; `canonicalSymplectic`: `ω = dθ` on `H × H` for a real inner product space
    `H`, strong when `H` is complete.

There is no `sorry`.

## Design

Mathlib's `ChartedSpace` and `StructureGroupoid` accept an arbitrary topological model space, so
Mathlib's chart machinery is reused directly. A manifold modeled on `E` is a `ChartedSpace E M`
with `HasGroupoid M (mbGroupoid E)`. Only the notion of smoothness changes: Mathlib's
`contDiffGroupoid` needs a normed model with corners, and this library replaces it with the
Michal–Bastiani calculus of lc-analysis.

## Build

```bash
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

The calculus library [lc-analysis](https://github.com/glaroton/lc-analysis) is a dependency (see
`lakefile.toml`).

## Using the library

Add the dependency to the `lakefile.toml` of a Lake project:

```toml
[[require]]
name = "lc-manifolds"
git = "https://github.com/glaroton/lc-manifolds"
rev = "main"
```

Then `import LocallyConvexManifolds` loads the whole library, and a single file is imported as
`LocallyConvexManifolds.Analytic`, etc. The declarations live in the namespace
`LocallyConvex.Manifolds`; those of lc-analysis, in `LocallyConvex.Analysis`.
